Industrial automation has mixed employment effects across developing economies, displacing specific roles while driving firm expansion.

The impact of industrial robots and artificial intelligence (AI) on manufacturing employment in developing economies is multifaceted. While technological adoption displaces routine production tasks, it also drives scale expansion and complementary labor demand, leading to net effects that diverge markedly across national contexts and skill strata.

### Mechanisms and Aggregate Trends

Evidence across emerging markets reveals that local automation does not always lead to immediate aggregate job losses. In global census analyses, operator and assembler roles expanded in many developing nations receiving foreign investment, contrasting with severe displacement in advanced economies (Maloney, 2019).

In China, provincial panel data demonstrate that industrial robotics and AI expand overall employment through scale economies and product innovation (Shen, 2024). Sectoral evaluations confirm that employment creation from robot adoption often outpaces substitution effects (Shao, 2023). Similarly, robotic integration enhances global value chain positioning and upstream service activity across industrial sectors (Yuan, 2023).

Conversely, multi-country enterprise surveys covering ten developing nations indicate that firms prioritizing new technology report reduced overall employment probabilities (Plumwongrot, 2022). Cross-country macroeconomic panels similarly show that robotization exerts downward pressure on manufacturing labor intensity (Zhu, 2023; Eder, 2025).

### Skill Disparities and Informality

- Routine task substitution suppresses hiring for low-skilled manual workers and machine operators while expanding technical positions (Jung, 2020; De Vries, 2020; Shao, 2023).
- Informal labor markets absorb displaced formal manufacturing workers, acting as an income cushion across Latin America (Brambilla, 2023).
- Premature deindustrialization risks emerge as foreign automation reduces import demand from developing exporters (Faber, 2020; Stemmler, 2023).
- Complementary skill premiums selectively benefit educated workers capable of operating digital manufacturing equipment (Plumwongrot, 2022; Fu, 2020; Wang, 2024).

In summary, while domestic industrial robot adoption can boost productivity and total manufacturing headcounts in expanding sectors, it consistently shifts labor demand toward higher-skilled roles and exposes developing exporters to job displacement driven by foreign reshoring.
